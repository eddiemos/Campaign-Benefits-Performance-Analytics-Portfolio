# Business Overview

## Context

The case study concerns a campaign-benefits reporting solution in a multi-market financial-services setting. The reporting brings campaign audience, product conversion, lending indicators, digital adoption and revenue-related measures together for stakeholder review.

All organization and market references have been generalized. Figures in the public sample data are synthetic.

## Business challenge

A campaign reporting process needs to connect operational activity with business outcomes. Stakeholders need to see not only how many customers were targeted, but also how many converted, which products were taken up, which channels were used and what financial measures were associated with those conversions.

## Reporting approach

The dashboard structure provides:
- An executive overview of headline performance indicators.
- Market-level comparison of targeting, conversion and lending outcomes.
- Daily and monthly monitoring of campaign activity.
- Campaign-category breakdowns.
- A glossary to explain business terminology and KPI interpretation.

## Questions the dashboard helps explore

1. How much of the targeted audience converted?
2. How does conversion vary across markets and campaign categories?
3. Which markets have high reach but relatively low conversion?
4. What share of conversions comes through digital routes?
5. How do campaign activity and conversions change over time?
6. How should operational measures be distinguished from financial outcomes?

## Interpretation guardrails

- A higher conversion rate does not by itself establish campaign causality.
- Disbursement and revenue are different measures.
- Projected revenue should not be presented as realized revenue.
- Comparisons should account for campaign mix, time windows and market context.
- The original report's exact source-system logic is not reproduced in this public case study.

## Privacy and publication

No original customer data, organization identifiers, proprietary campaign identifiers, connection strings or source PBIX file are included. Public examples use synthetic data and generic market labels.
